## 2026-07-29

**1. Customer zero**

He built a chat tool for his own 3am DM problem and used it privately for 3 months, sure it was too
specific to sell. It's since done $100k+ for other creators.

Specific is why people want it. The generic version already exists and nobody uses it.

**2. Shouting into the void**

"I suck at marketing" is almost never true.

One founder got their first 10 users by lurking in Facebook groups for a month, answering questions,
never mentioning their tool.

Cold outreach feels like a void because it's aimed at everyone.

The book is full of these →

**3. Burnout isn't a verdict**

Founder messaged hundreds of business owners, got nothing, and was ready to quit and finish his
degree.

Best reply in the thread: the burnout is coming from marketing, not the product. Two problems. Two
decisions.

More in the book →

**4. The churn nobody talks about**

A SaaS fixed how it handled failed payments and recovered more revenue than expected. No pricing
change. No marketing change.

Then found the real cause: their payment provider was broken for some countries.

Check the pipe before you optimize the pump.

**5. Your funnel's most expensive step**

They read your pricing. Checked integrations. Opened your docs. Clicked "Book a Demo."

Then you said: someone will get back to you.

One team deleted the wait and it changed everything about their inbound.

The book →

**6. Validation, actually**

"Fake the backend, not the frontend."

Deliver the real output by hand to 20-30 people before you build.

A landing page tests curiosity. Manual delivery tests whether the value lands.

Best advice I've read on pre-MVP validation. More in the book →

**7. Selling what you haven't built**

The demo that quietly implies the feature exists buys you a great call and a miserable
implementation.

Fix: name the missing piece plainly, put it in a separate pilot with a written acceptance test, and
make today's product stand alone.

More →

**8. Two clients is not a market**

Two freelance clients asked for an app, so he started building a multi-vertical white-label platform.

$1k MRR is a customer-count problem, not a calendar one. At $100/mo it's 10 customers.

Sell the same setup to a third with zero custom work first.

**9. Six years, $90,400, wrong customer**

300 cold DMs to find a cofounder. 150 investor emails a day for 8 months. Slept in his car for a $10k
check.

What finally made the wearable click? HYROX exploded and the market named his customer for him.

Notice, then sprint.

**10. Cost per lead is a lie by itself**

Agency: $50/day, 4 weeks, 4 leads, ~$350 each.
His own campaign: $30/day, 19 leads, ~$27 each.

But both used instant forms — bot-bait. A 20x gap is a question, not an answer.

Verify what a lead is before you celebrate.
